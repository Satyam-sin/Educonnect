1. Who Were the Marathas? Origins & Bhakti Foundation

  • Native Homeland: Native to the Deccan plateau (present-day Maharashtra), identified by the Marathi language (continuous literary history since the 12th century).
  • Early Political Background: In the 13th century, Maharashtra was ruled by the Yadava dynasty from Devagiri (Daulatabad). In the early 14th century, the Khilji Sultanate from Delhi overcame the Yadavas.
  • Bhakti Movement (7th–17th centuries): Saints rejected empty ritualism and composed devotional songs in local languages to unite society.
  • Key Maharashtra Bhakti Saints & Gurus: Sant Dnyaneshwar, Sant Namdev, Sant Tukaram, and Sant Ramdas translated the Upaniṣhads and Bhagavad Gītā into Marathi and fostered social organisation & political awareness (like the Sikh Gurus).

2. Foundation of Maratha Power & Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj

  • Birth & Early Life: Born in 1630 in the Bhonsle clan to Shahji and Jijabai; raised in his Pune jāgīr under Jijabai's guidance.
  • Vision of Swarājya (1646): At age 16, Shivaji launched military campaigns around Pune, capturing and strengthening neglected forts with the dream of sovereign self-rule (Swarājya).
  • Maratha Navy (1657): Founded a full-time navy to protect the west coast—unmatched by Bijapur (Adil Shahi) or the Mughals. Built naval forts like Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg.
  • Afzal Khan & Guerrilla Warfare (1659): Killed Bijapur general Afzal Khan at the foot of Pratapgad Fort using the wāgh nakh (tiger's claw), then routed his army via guerrilla warfare.
  • Night Raid on Shaista Khan: Raided Mughal general Shaista Khan's camp at night (like a modern surgical strike); Shaista Khan lost fingers and fled Maharashtra.
  • Sacking of Surat (1664 & 1670): Obtained ~₹1 crore from the wealthy Mughal port while sparing religious places and charitable merchant Mohandas Parekh's house. Reported in the London Gazette.
  • Treaty of Purandar (1665): Against Rajput general Jai Singh, Shivaji conceded defeat at Purandar Fort, ceded forts, and Sambhaji entered Mughal service.
  • Agra Escape (1666): Placed under house arrest by Aurangzeb after protesting an insult at court, Shivaji and Sambhaji escaped hidden inside fruit-and-sweet gift baskets.
  • Coronation at Raigad (1674): Crowned with full Vedic rites as 'Shri Raja Shiva Chhatrapati'; launched his own era, the Rājyābhiṣheka shaka.
  • Dakṣhiṇa-Digvijaya (1677): Conquered northern Tamil Nadu and parts of Karnataka; forbade the Dutch from slave trading.
  • Passing & Inspiration (1680): Passed away at age 50 due to fever. Inspired Bundela prince Chhatrasal (independent Bundelkhand) and Hindi poet Bhushan.

3. The Marathas After Shivaji & Pan-Indian Expansion

  • Chhatrapati Sambhaji: Resisted Aurangzeb after Bijapur and Golconda fell; captured, brutally tortured, and executed, followed by the fall of Raigad.
  • Chhatrapati Rajaram & Maharani Tarabai (1682–1707): Rajaram shifted resistance to Gingee (Tamil Nadu); Tarabai launched northward offensives. Aurangzeb died in the Deccan (1707) unable to subdue them.
  • Peshwas & Pan-Indian Reach: Decentralised confederacy led by Peshwa ('Prime Minister') — Bajirao I & Nanasaheb Peshwa (under Chhatrapati Shahu Maharaj) brought Delhi (1754) and briefly Lahore, Attock, and Peshawar under Maratha control.
  • Panipat (1761) & Delhi Recapture (1771): Recovered rapidly under Peshwa Madhavrao I; Mahadji Shinde (Scindia) recaptured Delhi in 1771 and held it for three decades.
  • Three Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818): Marathas won the First War (1775–1782); Nana Phadnavis formed the first pan-Indian anti-British alliance with Hyder Ali and the Nizam of Hyderabad. Internal disunity led to defeat in the Third War (1818).
CHS Exam Must-Know In effect, the British took India from the Marathas more than from the Mughals or any other Indian power!

4. Civilian Administration, Aṣhṭa Pradhāna & Revenue System

  • Centralised Governance: Abolished hereditary posts & land assignments; paid cash salaries from the treasury and periodically transferred officials.
  • Welfare & Ecology: Pensions to fallen soldiers' widows & military posts to their sons; forbade cutting fruit trees like mango and jackfruit.
  • Coinage & Taxes: Devanagari gold/copper coins; bilingual Gaṇapatī-Pantapradhān rupee (Devanagari + Persian) by Patwardhans; Chauth (25%) and Sardeshmukhi (+10%) levied for protection.

5. Military, Forts, Maritime Supremacy, Judiciary & Trade

  • Three Wings: Infantry, Cavalry, and Navy. Cavalry had Bārgīrs (state-funded) and Shiledārs (self-funded).
  • Weapons & Modernisation: Swords, lances, guns, rockets from Shivaji's era, metal tube rockets by 1770; Mahadji Shinde built a large European-style disciplined army.
  • Forts & Navy: Ādnyāpatra calls forts "the core of the state". Admiral Kanhoji Angre challenged European cartaz naval passes. In 1665, Muscat's ruler retreated upon learning Shivaji had 100+ ships.
  • Judiciary & Trade: Moderate in capital punishment; local panchāyats delivered justice; kotwāls policed cities (Pune, Indore). Ships traded with Mocha (Yemen), Muscat (Oman), and Malacca (Malaysia); river ferries operated in Odisha.

6. Cultural Revival, Mighty Maratha Women & Thanjavur

  • Sanskrit Seal & Language: Shivaji's Sanskrit seal departed from Persian norms; Rājya-Vyavahāra-Koṣha replaced Persian diplomatic words with Sanskrit/Marathi terms. Used saffron flag & Moḍī script. Bhonsles of Nagpur revived Lord Jagannath worship at Puri.
  • Tarabai & Ahilyabai Holkar: Tarabai led northward expansion; Ahilyabai ruled Indore for 30 years, rebuilt Kashi Vishwanath & Somnath, and revitalised Maheshwar handloom.
  • Thanjavur Marathas (Ekoji & Serfoji II): Ekoji conquered Thanjavur (Tamil Nadu). Serfoji II wrote Devendra Kuravanji, patronised Carnatic music & Bharatanatyam, founded Dhanwantari Mahal (free medical centre), set up the first native printing press, and inscribed Bhonsle history on the Brihadishwara temple wall.

8. Key Terms Glossary

SwarājyaSovereign self-rule envisioned by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (political, economic & cultural).
JāgīrLand grant given by a ruler to a noble/soldier in reward for service, with tax collection rights.
Wāgh Nakh'Tiger's claw' concealed hand weapon used by Shivaji against Afzal Khan at Pratapgad.
Rājyābhiṣheka ShakaNew calendar era launched by Shivaji upon his 1674 coronation at Raigad.
Chauth & SardeshmukhiMaratha taxes: 25% (Chauth) + additional 10% (Sardeshmukhi) levied for protection.
Bārgīrs vs. ShiledārsBārgīrs = state-funded cavalry; Shiledārs = self-funded cavalry soldiers.
CartazNaval trade pass forced by the Portuguese on Indian ships, challenged by Kanhoji Angre.
Moḍī ScriptCursive form of Devanagari used as the main script for Maratha official correspondence.
