1. Motion, Speed & Acceleration

Speed

SI: m/s
Speed (v) = Distance (d) / Time (t)

Measures how fast an object covers distance. Cover 'S' in the D-S-T triangle to get D/T.

Example: A bus covers 120 km in 2 h → Speed = 120 / 2 = 60 km/h.

Average Speed

SI: m/s
Avg Speed = Total Distance / Total Time

Used when speed changes during a journey. Never just average two speeds if times differ!

Example: 40 km in 1 h + 80 km in 2 h → 120 / 3 = 40 km/h.

km/h to m/s Shortcut

Conversion
v (m/s) = v (km/h) × (5 / 18)

Since 1 km = 1000 m and 1 h = 3600 s, 1000/3600 simplifies directly to 5/18. Reverse with 18/5.

Example: 72 km/h × 5/18 = 20 m/s.

Acceleration

SI: m/s²
a = (v − u) / t

Rate of change of velocity per second. Here u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, t = time.

Example: Velocity rises from 4 m/s to 20 m/s in 4 s → a = (20−4)/4 = 4 m/s².

2. Force, Weight, Pressure, Work & Energy

Newton's 2nd Law (Force)

SI: Newton (N)
F = m × a

Force equals mass (kg) multiplied by acceleration (m/s²). 1 N gives 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

Example: A 5 kg box accelerated at 3 m/s² needs F = 5 × 3 = 15 N.

Weight of an Object

SI: Newton (N)
W = m × g

Gravitational pull on a mass. On Earth g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (or 10 m/s²); on Moon g is 1/6th of Earth.

Example: Mass 10 kg on Earth → W = 10 × 9.8 = 98 N.

Pressure on Solids

SI: Pascal (Pa)
P = Thrust (F) / Area (A)

Force acting perpendicularly per unit area. Smaller contact area produces much higher pressure (e.g., sharp knife).

Example: Force 200 N on 0.5 m² → P = 200 / 0.5 = 400 Pa.

Liquid Column Pressure

SI: Pascal (Pa)
P = h × ρ × g

Pressure inside a liquid increases with depth (h) and liquid density (ρ), independent of container shape.

Example: At 2 m depth in water (ρ = 1000, g = 10) → P = 20,000 Pa.

Work Done

SI: Joule (J)
W = Force (F) × Displacement (d)

Work is done only when a force moves an object in the direction of the force. 1 J = 1 N·m.

Example: Pushing with 30 N over 4 m → W = 30 × 4 = 120 J.

Kinetic & Potential Energy

SI: Joule (J)
KE = ½ m v² | PE = m g h

KE is energy of motion (doubles mass → 2× KE; doubles speed → 4× KE). PE is energy stored due to height h.

Example: 2 kg ball moving at 3 m/s → KE = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.

Mechanical Power

SI: Watt (W)
Power (P) = Work (W) / Time (t)

Rate of doing work per second. 1 Watt = 1 Joule/second. 1 Horsepower (HP) = 746 W.

Example: 600 J work done in 10 s → P = 600 / 10 = 60 W.

Net Resultant Force

SI: Newton (N)
F_net = F₁ + F₂ (same) | |F₁ − F₂| (opp)

Forces in the same direction add up; forces in opposite directions subtract. Balanced forces give F_net = 0.

Example: 25 N right vs 15 N left → F_net = 10 N right.

3. Density, Relative Density & Buoyancy

Density

SI: kg/m³
ρ = Mass (m) / Volume (V)

Measures how tightly matter is packed. Water has density 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³.

Example: Mass 500 g, Volume 250 cm³ → ρ = 2 g/cm³ = 2000 kg/m³.

Relative Density (RD)

Unitless
RD = ρ_substance / ρ_water

Ratio of two densities, so units cancel completely! If RD > 1, the object sinks in water; if RD < 1, it floats.

Example: Iron ρ = 7800 kg/m³ → RD = 7800 / 1000 = 7.8 (sinks).

Archimedes' Buoyant Force

SI: Newton (N)
F_b = Weight of Displaced Fluid

Upward thrust exerted by a fluid equals the weight of the fluid pushed aside by the submerged object.

Example: Object displaces 0.5 kg water → Upthrust ≈ 0.5 × 9.8 = 4.9 N.

Apparent Weight in Water

SI: Newton (N)
W_apparent = W_air − F_buoyant

Objects feel lighter inside water because upward buoyant force reduces the downward pull.

Example: Stone weighs 20 N in air, upthrust 6 N → 14 N in water.

4. Electricity, Ohm's Law & Joule Heating

Electric Current

SI: Ampere (A)
I = Charge (Q) / Time (t)

Rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. Measured by an Ammeter connected in series.

Example: 20 C charge flows in 4 s → I = 20 / 4 = 5 A.

Ohm's Law

SI: Volt (V)
V = I × R

At constant temperature, voltage (V) across a wire is directly proportional to current (I). R is Resistance (Ω).

Example: I = 2 A through R = 6 Ω → V = 2 × 6 = 12 V.

Series & Parallel Resistance

SI: Ohm (Ω)
R_s = R₁ + R₂ | 1/R_p = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂

Series increases total resistance; Parallel decreases total resistance below the smallest resistor.

Example: Two 6 Ω resistors: Series = 12 Ω, Parallel = 3 Ω.

Joule's Law of Heating

SI: Joule (J)
H = I² × R × t

Heat produced in a wire grows with square of current (I²), resistance (R), and time (t). Used in heaters & fuses.

Example: I = 2 A, R = 5 Ω, t = 10 s → H = 4 × 5 × 10 = 200 J.

Electric Power

SI: Watt (W)
P = V × I = I²R = V² / R

Electrical energy consumed per second. 1000 W = 1 kW.

Example: 220 V bulb drawing 0.5 A → P = 220 × 0.5 = 110 W.

Commercial Electricity Unit

1 Unit (kWh)
1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J

Your home electricity bill counts 'Units' in kilowatt-hours (kWh), a unit of energy, NOT power!

Example: A 2 kW geyser run for 3 hours uses 2 × 3 = 6 Units.

5. Light: Mirrors & Lenses (Optics)

Law of Reflection

Degrees (°)
∠i = ∠r

Angle of incidence (between incident ray and normal) always equals angle of reflection.

Example: If ∠i = 35°, then ∠r = 35° and angle between rays = 70°.

Glancing Angle with Mirror

Degrees (°)
θ = 90° − ∠i

Normal is always 90° to the mirror surface. CHS loves giving the surface angle to trap students!

Example: Ray makes 30° with mirror → ∠i = 90° − 30° = 60°.

Multiple Images (Two Mirrors)

Count
n = (360° / θ) − 1

Number of images formed when two plane mirrors are placed at angle θ (when 360°/θ is even).

Example: Mirrors at 90° → (360/90) − 1 = 3 images; at 60° → 5 images.

Focal Length & Radius

SI: metre (m)
f = R / 2

For spherical mirrors (concave/convex), focal length (f) is half the radius of curvature (R).

Example: Concave mirror with R = 24 cm → f = 12 cm.

Power of a Lens

SI: Dioptre (D)
P = 1 / f (in metres)

Convex (converging) lens has positive (+) power; Concave (diverging) lens has negative (−) power.

Example: Convex lens with f = 50 cm (0.5 m) → P = 1 / 0.5 = +2 D.

Linear Magnification

Unitless
m = Height of Image (h′) / Height of Object (h)

If |m| > 1 image is magnified; if |m| < 1 diminished; if |m| = 1 same size (plane mirror).

Example: Object 4 cm produces 12 cm image → m = 12 / 4 = 3×.

6. Heat, Temperature & Sound Waves

Celsius to Fahrenheit

°F / °C
°F = (9/5 × °C) + 32

Converts clinical or weather temperatures. Note that −40°C equals exactly −40°F!

Example: 37°C = (9/5 × 37) + 32 = 98.6°F (normal human body).

Celsius to Kelvin (SI)

SI: Kelvin (K)
K = °C + 273.15

Kelvin is the SI unit of temperature (never write a degree ° sign before K!). 0 K is Absolute Zero.

Example: 27°C = 27 + 273 = 300 K.

Frequency & Time Period

SI: Hertz (Hz)
f = 1 / T | T = 1 / f

Frequency is number of vibrations per second; Time Period (T) is seconds for 1 complete oscillation.

Example: Pendulum makes 50 oscillations in 10 s → f = 5 Hz, T = 0.2 s.

Wave Speed Formula

SI: m/s
v = f × λ

Speed of sound equals frequency (Hz) times wavelength (λ in metres). Sound travels fastest in solids!

Example: f = 200 Hz, λ = 1.7 m → v = 200 × 1.7 = 340 m/s.

7. CHS Maths: Mensuration & Geometry

Circle Area & Circumference

π ≈ 22/7 or 3.14
Area = πr² | Circumference = 2πr

Radius r = Diameter / 2. Semicircle perimeter is πr + 2r (don't forget the straight diameter!).

Example: r = 7 cm → Area = (22/7)×7×7 = 154 cm², C = 44 cm.

Trapezium & Rhombus Area

sq. units
Trap = ½(a+b)h | Rhombus = ½ × d₁ × d₂

a and b are parallel sides of trapezium; d₁ and d₂ are perpendicular diagonals of a rhombus.

Example: Rhombus diagonals 10 cm & 8 cm → Area = ½×10×8 = 40 cm².

Cube Surface Area & Volume

m² / m³
LSA = 4a² | TSA = 6a² | Vol = a³

All 6 faces of a cube are identical squares of side a. Diagonal of cube = a√3.

Example: Side a = 5 cm → TSA = 150 cm², Volume = 125 cm³.

Cuboid Surface Area & Volume

m² / m³
TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl) | Vol = l × b × h

Area of 4 walls (LSA) = 2h(l + b). Very frequent in CHS painting/room-flooring problems.

Example: l=4, b=3, h=2 m → Vol = 24 m³, TSA = 52 m².

Cylinder Area & Volume

m² / m³
CSA = 2πrh | TSA = 2πr(r+h) | V = πr²h

CSA is the curved label area; TSA adds both circular top and bottom lids (2πr²).

Example: r = 7 cm, h = 10 cm → V = (22/7)×49×10 = 1540 cm³.

Polygon Interior Angles & Pythagoras

Geometry
Sum = (n − 2) × 180° | h² = p² + b²

Sum of exterior angles of ANY convex polygon is ALWAYS 360°. Right triangle satisfies h² = p² + b².

Example: Pentagon (n=5) interior sum = 3 × 180° = 540°.

8. CHS Maths: Algebra, Interest & Exponents

Square Identities

Algebra
(a±b)² = a² ± 2ab + b² | a²−b² = (a+b)(a−b)

Fastest mental-math shortcut for squaring numbers like 103² or computing 99² − 98² in 2 seconds.

Example: 52² − 48² = (52+48)(52−48) = 100 × 4 = 400.

Simple Interest (SI)

Finance
SI = (P × R × T) / 100 | Amount = P + SI

P = Principal, R = Annual Rate %, T = Time in years. Interest stays identical every year.

Example: P = ₹2000, R = 10%, T = 3 yrs → SI = ₹600.

Compound Interest (CI)

Finance
A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ | CI = A − P

Interest earns interest each period. For half-yearly compounding, use R/2 and 2n!

Example: ₹1000 at 10% for 2 yrs → A = 1000×(1.1)² = ₹1210, CI = ₹210.

Laws of Exponents

Exponents
aᵐ·aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ | aᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ | a⁰ = 1 | a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ

Any non-zero number raised to power 0 equals 1. Negative power means reciprocal.

Example: 2³ × 2⁴ = 2⁷ = 128; 5⁻² = 1/25.
