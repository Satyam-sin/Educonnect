01

Who Were the Marathas? Origins & Bhakti Foundation

Homeland & Identity

  • Homeland: Native to the Deccan plateau (present-day Maharashtra), speaking the Marathi language.
  • Yadava Rule: In the 13th century, Maharashtra was ruled by the Yadava dynasty from Devagiri (Daulatabad).
  • Sultanate Conquest: In the early 14th century, the Khilji Sultanate defeated the Yadavas.
  • Resilience: Despite political upheaval, Marathi language, culture, and self-respect stayed strong.

Bhakti Movement Foundation

  • Time Period: Flourished across Maharashtra from the 7th to 17th centuries.
  • Great Saints: Sant Dnyaneshwar, Sant Namdev, Sant Tukaram, and Sant Ramdas led the spiritual revival.
  • Key Contribution: Translated the Upanishads and Bhagavad Gita into everyday Marathi.
  • Social Impact: Promoted devotion, equality, and cultural unity — laying the moral base for Maratha self-rule.
CHS Quick Fact: The Bhakti movement gave Maharashtra cultural unity long before political unity under Shivaji Maharaj.
02

Foundation of Maratha Power & Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj

Early Life & Vision of Swarajya

  • Birth (1630): Born at Shivneri Fort in the Bhonsle clan to Shahji and Jijabai.
  • Upbringing: Raised in Pune under Jijabai and trusted mentors; learned statecraft, values, and warfare.
  • Swarajya (1646): At just age 16, began capturing neglected hill forts around Pune for sovereign self-rule.
  • Maratha Navy (1657): Built a permanent navy to guard the west coast; erected sea forts like Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg.

Brilliant Battles & Daring Escapes

  • Afzal Khan (1659): Defeated Bijapur general Afzal Khan at Pratapgad using the Wagh Nakh (tiger claws).
  • Shaista Khan (1663): Led a daring night raid on Mughal commander Shaista Khan's camp in Pune, forcing his retreat.
  • Sack of Surat (1664 & 1670): Raided the wealthy Mughal port of Surat; spared religious places and poor citizens.
  • Treaty of Purandar (1665): Signed with Mughal general Jai Singh; ceded 23 forts temporarily.
  • Agra Escape (1666): Outwitted Aurangzeb's house arrest by escaping hidden inside large fruit and sweet baskets.

Coronation & Southern Conquest

  • Coronation (1674): Crowned Chhatrapati at Raigad Fort, starting the Rajyabhisheka Shaka calendar era.
  • Dakshina Digvijaya (1677–1680): Conquered territories across northern Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, securing deep strategic depth.
  • Passing (1680): Passed away at Raigad at age 50, leaving behind a well-governed, self-reliant kingdom.
03

The Marathas After Shivaji & Pan-Indian Expansion

Resistance Against Aurangzeb

  • Chhatrapati Sambhaji (1680–1689): Fought Aurangzeb bravely; captured and executed in 1689 after refusing to surrender.
  • Chhatrapati Rajaram (1689–1700): Shifted resistance to Gingee Fort (Tamil Nadu), keeping Mughal armies tied down.
  • Maharani Tarabai (1700–1707): Led fierce mobile warfare into Mughal northern territories; Aurangzeb died in 1707 without conquering the Marathas.

Peshwa Era & Pan-Indian Empire

  • Rise of Peshwas: Under Peshwa Bajirao I and Nanasaheb Peshwa, the Marathas expanded rapidly across India.
  • Maximum Extent (1758): Stretched from Attock / Peshawar in the northwest to Bengal and Thanjavur in the south.
  • Third Battle of Panipat (1761): Suffered a heavy defeat against Ahmad Shah Abdali, but recovered within a decade.
  • Revival (1771): Under Peshwa Madhavrao I and Mahadji Shinde, recaptured Delhi and restored dominance.
  • Anglo-Maratha Wars (1775–1818): Fought three major wars with the British; internal disunity and British artillery ended Maratha rule in 1818.
04

Civilian Administration, Ashta Pradhan & Revenue System

Clean & Centralised Governance

  • Abolished Hereditary Posts: Shivaji ended hereditary jagirs so officials stayed loyal to the state, not local landlords.
  • Cash Salaries & Transfers: Paid officers directly in cash from the treasury and transferred them regularly to prevent corruption.
  • Welfare Support: Provided pensions to widows of fallen soldiers and offered jobs to their sons.

Maratha Revenue Taxes

  • Chauth (25%): One-fourth of revenue collected from neighbouring provinces in return for protection from raids.
  • Sardeshmukhi (10%): An additional 10% levy claimed by the Maratha king as hereditary chief headman (Sardeshmukh).
  • Farmer Protection: Soldiers were strictly ordered never to harm crops, trees, or villagers during marches.

Ashta Pradhan Mandal (Council of 8 Ministers)

2. Amatya (Majumdar)Finance & Revenue Minister

05

Military, Forts, Maritime Supremacy, Judiciary & Trade

Army & Guerrilla Warfare

  • Three Wings: Infantry, Cavalry, and a dedicated Navy.
  • Bargirs: Regular state cavalry whose horses and weapons were supplied by the Maratha treasury.
  • Shiledars: Cavalrymen who brought their own horses and equipment.
  • Guerrilla Warfare (Ganimi Kava): Used Sahyadri hills, speed, and surprise ambushes to defeat giant Mughal armies.

Forts, Navy & Justice

  • Forts — Core of State: Over 300 forts; Ramachandrapant Amatya's Adnyapatra calls forts "the very essence of the state".
  • Kanhoji Angre (18th Century): Legendary Maratha Admiral who defeated European navies and forced them to buy Maratha trade passes (dastaks).
  • Local Justice: Village Panchayats settled local disputes; Kotwals maintained law and order in cities like Pune.
  • Maritime Trade: Shivaji built merchant ships that traded directly with Mocha (Yemen), Muscat (Oman), and Malacca.
06

Cultural Revival, Mighty Maratha Women & Thanjavur

Language, Seal & Cultural Pride

  • Rajya Vyavahara Kosha: Dictionary commissioned by Shivaji replacing Persian administrative words with Sanskrit/Marathi equivalents.
  • Sanskrit Royal Seal: Declared that Shivaji's Swarajya would grow like the waxing crescent moon for the welfare of all people.
  • Modi Script: Fast cursive script widely used for official Maratha correspondence and records.
  • Religious Tolerance: Protected temples, mosques, and churches equally; welcomed capable officers of all faiths.

Mighty Maratha Women Leaders

  • Rajmata Jijabai: Guided young Shivaji with courage, ethics, and the vision of Swarajya.
  • Maharani Tarabai: Brilliant strategist who led the Maratha empire against Aurangzeb (1700–1707).
  • Ahilyabai Holkar (Indore): Ruled Malwa for 30 years; rebuilt Kashi Vishwanath and Somnath temples, ghats, wells, and promoted Maheshwari sarees.

Thanjavur Marathas (Tamil Nadu)

  • Founder: Established by Shivaji's half-brother Ekoji (Venkoji) in the late 17th century.
  • Raja Serfoji II: Built the world-famous Saraswati Mahal Library, set up India's early Marathi/Devanagari printing press, and supported Carnatic music, Bharatanatyam, and free medical care (Dhanwantari Mahal).

8. Key Terms Glossary

SwarajyaSovereign self-rule envisioned by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (political, economic & cultural).
JagirLand grant given by a ruler to a noble/soldier in reward for service, with tax collection rights.
Wagh Nakh'Tiger's claw' concealed hand weapon used by Shivaji against Afzal Khan at Pratapgad.
Rajyabhisheka ShakaNew calendar era launched by Shivaji upon his 1674 coronation at Raigad.
Chauth & SardeshmukhiMaratha taxes: 25% (Chauth) + additional 10% (Sardeshmukhi) levied for protection.
Bargirs vs. ShiledarsBargirs = state-funded cavalry; Shiledars = self-funded cavalry soldiers.
CartazNaval trade pass forced by the Portuguese on Indian ships, challenged by Kanhoji Angre.
Modi ScriptCursive form of Devanagari used as the main script for Maratha official correspondence.
